## Asia V-Reversal — When It Happens (NQ)

Everyone talks about Asia running one way then reversing for the rest of the night. I wanted the actual number — how often NQ carves a real V-reversal between 18:00 and 00:00 ET, and what hour the turn usually lands. 10 years of data.

### How I set it up

- NQ continuous front-month, 1-minute bars
- 2016-04-19 → 2026-05-12, 2,577 nights
- A real V-reversal = a down-leg of ≥0.15% into a low, then a rally of ≥0.15% back out, with the low *not* in the first 3 minutes (so I'm not counting opening noise)
- The "manipulation" is the sweep-low itself

### How often it actually happens

- **1,330 nights** had a real V-reversal — **51.6%** of Asia sessions.
- Median rally out of the low: **46 points**.
- The hour buckets below cover **78.6%** of them.

So just over half the time. The other nights either trend straight, chop, or do the opposite (up then down). A clean V is the base case, not something you can count on.

### What hour the turn lands (2h blocks, ET)

Of the 1,330 reversal nights:

- **18:00–20:00 ET** (00:00–02:00 Paris): **34.4%** (458)
- **20:00–22:00 ET** (02:00–04:00 Paris): **45.7%** (608) — the busy block
- **22:00–00:00 ET** (04:00–06:00 Paris): **19.8%** (264)

Single busiest hour is **20:00 ET** (23.7% of all reversals), 21:00 ET right behind (22.0%). The turn mostly happens in the middle of Asia, not at the open and not at the very end. By 23:00 ET it's basically over (6.5%).

So if you're watching for it, your window is roughly 20:00–22:00 ET. That tells you *where to look*, not what to do — the sweep-low still has to form and hold first.
