## Opex Week — Which Sessions Compress? (NQ)

Everyone says options-expiration week pins price and kills the range. I never fully bought that it kills *every* session equally, so I checked it — Asia, London and NY AM, Opex week vs the rest of the month, over 10 years of NQ.

### How I set it up

- NQ continuous front-month
- 2016-04-19 → 2026-12-31, 7,748 sessions
- Opex week = Monday–Friday of the third-Friday week
- Range in NQ points; also ATR-normalized (range ÷ its own trailing 14-session average) to strip out regime drift
- Sessions: Asia (18:00–00:00 ET), London (02:00–05:00 ET), NY AM (09:30–11:00 ET)
- Left volume out — the quarterly contract roll lands on Opex and wrecks it

### Range: Opex vs the rest of the month

| Session | Opex avg range | Non-Opex avg range | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asia | 62.5 pts | 72.3 pts | **−13.6%** |
| London | 61.6 pts | 62.1 pts | **−0.8%** |
| NY AM | 113.1 pts | 120.7 pts | **−6.3%** |

The compression is real but it's lopsided. **Asia** is the one that gets pinned — almost 14% off its range. **NY AM** tightens a bit (−6.3%). **London barely moves** — its Opex range is basically the same as any other week (−0.8%).

### London is the exception

London is the session that doesn't compress in Opex. While Asia sits on its hands waiting for expiration and the US cash session trades a little tighter, London keeps delivering its normal range. In a week where the tape is supposed to be dead, London is where the movement still shows up — and it held across 2022–2025.

Worth saying: this is average and median range, not direction. A normal-range session can still go nowhere. Treat it as "where range survives in Opex," nothing more.
